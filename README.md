A Moment in the Cosmos is a daily astronomy site that features one real photograph of the universe each day. At midnight UTC it changes to a new image, the same for every visitor, and cycles through four categories: nebulae, galaxies, stars, and night-sky landscapes over observatories. The collection holds 1,455 photographs from ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, ESO and NSF NOIRLab, all released under open Creative Commons licences. Each one was reviewed by eye before it went in, and there are no illustrations, simulations or AI-generated images. The site has a print-inspired "observatory almanac" design with a subtle constellation background.

Every photograph comes with an original story written from the source agency's caption and then fact-checked against it. Alongside it, visitors can see the object's size and distance, which telescope and filters were used, what the colours represent, and how the image differs from what the eye would see. The full credit and source links are shown too. For selected images there's a Hubble vs Webb slider for comparing the same object in both telescopes, and guided "look closer" tours that zoom into features the caption describes. An immersive viewer lets people pan and zoom through the full-resolution file.

Beyond the daily pick, visitors can browse the collection at random, save favourites, and scroll back through an archive of every past daily feature. The "On this day" section lets anyone pick a calendar date and see what happened in astronomy on it, from 493 researched events such as discoveries, telescope launches and famous astronomers. Each event links to its source and to related photos in the collection. The section also shows every photograph the agencies published on that date in past years, so each date has at least three images to explore.
