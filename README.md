Welcome to **A Moment in the Cosmos**, a quiet place to look up, slow down, and spend a little time with the universe.

Every day at midnight UTC, a new photograph appears for everyone to discover together. You might arrive to find a nebula glowing with newly formed stars, a galaxy suspended millions of light-years away, a brilliant stellar remnant, or the Milky Way rising above a remote observatory.

The collection holds **1,455 real astronomical photographs** from ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, ESO, and NSF NOIRLab. Every image was reviewed by eye before being added. There are no illustrations, simulations, or AI-generated images. Each view comes from real observations of space or the night sky.

The daily photograph is only the beginning.

Each feature tells the story of what you’re seeing, using information from the original astronomy agency and checked against the source. You can find out how far away the object is, how large it is, which telescope captured it, which filters were used, and what the colors in the image actually represent. You can also see how the scene might differ if you were somehow able to view it with your own eyes.

Some images reward an even closer look. **“Look Closer”** tours guide you through details hidden across the frame, while the full-resolution viewer lets you pan and zoom freely through the original image. For selected objects, a **Hubble vs Webb** slider lets you move between two views of the same place and see how different wavelengths reveal different parts of the story.

There’s no single path through the collection. Wander at random, save the photographs you want to return to, or explore the archive and revisit every daily feature that has appeared before.

You can also turn the calendar backward with **“On This Day.”** Choose any date and you’ll find discoveries, telescope launches, milestones, and astronomers connected to that moment in history. The collection includes **493 researched events**, each linked to its source and to photographs that help bring the story to life.

The calendar also gathers images published by the agencies on that same date in years past, giving every day its own small gallery of the universe.

Stay for a photograph. Follow a story. Get lost in the details.

Then come back tomorrow and see what the cosmos has waiting.
