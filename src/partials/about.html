About me, and this site

Hi, I’m Joe! Thanks for stopping by my little corner of the internet!

I’ve been fascinated by space for as long as I can remember. There’s something about looking up at the night sky and realizing just how much is out there that never gets old. Somewhere between growing up, going to work, and keeping up with everyday life, though, I realized I’d stopped making time for something I used to love so much. I think 10-year-old me would be pretty disappointed…

So! 20-something years later, I decided to put this little website together. Partly as a promise to my younger self, but also as a reminder that no matter how hectic life gets on terra firma, there’s still an unfathomable expanse of incredible things happening above.

One thing that always bothered me was how so many beautiful photographs of space get their moment in the spotlight, only to be tucked away in an archive the next day. I wanted to give them a little more love! Hopefully, you’ll discover something that makes you stop and appreciate the universe for a moment.

How each photo is picked

Every day at midnight UTC, three new photographs go up for everyone to enjoy: a galaxy, a night sky, and either a nebula or a star. Vote for your favorite, and the next day you’ll find out which one won! And with at least 1,600 photos, it’ll be quite a while before you see the same one again.

Photos are sourced from ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, ESO, NSF NOIRLab, and NASA. I’ve personally gone through every photograph before adding it, and the stories you’ll read are based on the original captions provided by the observatories themselves.

The daily archive begins from the day this little project launched, so you can always revisit any photographs you might’ve missed!

A few things I care about

  • Only real photographs: telescope images and night-sky photography. No illustrations or simulations.
  • Nothing is upscaled. If you want to get really close, Immersive view will fetch the observatory’s full-resolution file.
  • The Hubble vs Webb sliders use ESA/Webb’s own lined-up pairs, so you’ll only see one where they made it. The guided tours stop at things the observatory itself points out.
  • When an observatory singled a picture out, say in its Top 100 or as a Picture of the Week, I note it under “Recognition”.

Credit where it’s due

None of these pictures are mine. Images from ESA/Hubble, ESA/Webb, ESO and NSF NOIRLab are shared under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, and NASA’s aren’t under US copyright, though NASA asks to be credited. Every photograph carries its full credit, and the Download menu will copy it for you.

Every photograph also has its own link, so if one stops you in your tracks, send it to someone.
